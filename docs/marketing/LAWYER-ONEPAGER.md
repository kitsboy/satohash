## In one paragraph

Satohash lets a person prove a file existed at a precise moment in time — **without handing the file to us.** The document is hashed locally (SHA-256, Web Crypto) in the browser; only the 64-character fingerprint ever leaves the device. That fingerprint is committed to the Bitcoin blockchain through the open OpenTimestamps (OTS) standard. The result is a portable `.ots` proof that any third party can verify forever with free, open tools — **no account, no KYC, no trust in Satohash required after the proof is issued.**

> **We can't read it. We can't lose it. We never had it.**

## Why it matters (the problem, plainly)

Proving *"what existed, when, unaltered"* has become harder, not easier — generative AI, deepfakes, and centralized platforms that store — and can alter — your documents. Traditional notarization is slow and paper-era. Centralized e-sign platforms demand your confidential files, on a subscription, on their say-so. Satohash turns existence into math on Bitcoin: a public, decentralized, battle-tested ledger that no single company can buy, breach, or shut down.

## What Satohash is — and what it is not (the honest line)

| Satohash **is** | Satohash **is not** |
|---|---|
| Client-side SHA-256 hashing; only the hash is submitted | A notary, or a replacement for notarial acts where law requires them |
| OpenTimestamps calendar aggregation → Bitcoin block | An electronic-signature service |
| A portable `.ots` proof, independently verifiable forever | A regulated Time Stamping Authority (TSA) |
| **Zero-knowledge:** document bytes never leave the device | A qualified electronic time stamp (QETS) under eIDAS |

**The defensible claim (counsel-approved framing):**
> "Satohash creates an electronic record of existence-at-a-time that is independently verifiable via the public Bitcoin blockchain. ESIGN and UETA provide that electronic records are not denied legal effect merely because they are electronic. Whether a specific stamp is admitted, and the weight it receives, depends on the rules of evidence and the specific proceeding."

**And the honest caveat we lead with:**
> "A Satohash stamp proves a file existed, in a specific form, by a specific time — mathematically, and verifiable by anyone, forever. It is strong evidence. It is not a notary, a signature, or a regulated timestamp authority."
